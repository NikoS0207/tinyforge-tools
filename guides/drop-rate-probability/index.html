Drop rate probability without the guesswork

A 1% drop rate does not mean everyone gets the item after 100 attempts.

The core formula

If each attempt is independent and the drop chance is p, the chance of missing once is 1 − p. Missing n times in a row is (1 − p)^n. Therefore the chance of at least one drop after n attempts is 1 − (1 − p)^n.

Why “expected attempts” is not a guarantee

For a 1% drop, the average waiting time is 100 attempts, but some players get it immediately and others can go far beyond 100. Designing around the average alone hides the painful tail of the distribution.

Useful confidence thresholds

Instead of only asking for the average, ask how many attempts are needed for 50%, 90%, 95% or 99% of players to have seen at least one success. That is much closer to the player-experience question designers care about.

When to add pity

If long dry streaks damage trust or progression, a soft or hard pity system can intentionally trim the tail. That should be modeled separately from a fixed independent drop chance.
